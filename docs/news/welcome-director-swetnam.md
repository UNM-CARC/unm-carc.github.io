# Welcome our new CARC Director, Tyson Swetnam

<p class="carc-byline">By CARC staff</p>

The University of New Mexico’s Center for Advanced Research Computing (CARC) 
welcomes Tyson Swetnam as its new director. Swetnam began the role on July 1, 2026, 
and also serves as an associate professor in UNM’s Department of Computer Science. 
His background combines environmental research, data science, and the development 
of computing resources that help researchers work with large datasets.
Before joining UNM, Swetnam served as a research associate professor and director of 
open science initiatives at the University of Arizona’s Arizona Institute for Artificial 
Intelligence & Society. He earned his doctorate in watershed management at the 
University of Arizona, where his research focused on remote sensing and spatial 
analysis.
Swetnam’s path into research computing began with work outdoors. Early in his career, 
he worked as a wildland firefighter at Saguaro National Park and later as a fire 
management specialist on the Coronado National Forest. His research on forests, 
wildfire, and watersheds involved increasingly large datasets, leading him toward data 
science and the computing systems needed to support that work.
That experience has grown into a focus on helping researchers access and use 
computational tools across fields such as biology, ecology, agriculture, and earth 
science. Alongside his research, Swetnam has helped develop training in open science 
and digital research skills, supporting researchers in learning how to organize, share, 
and reproduce their work.
At CARC, Swetnam’s priorities include expanding access to research data storage, 
developing shared artificial intelligence services, and continuing to improve highperformance computing and cloud resources. In a recent post about joining UNM, he 
described his goal of making advanced computing more accessible to students and 
researchers throughout New Mexico, including those at tribal colleges and rural 
institutions.
Swetnam also recognized CARC’s connection to the arts and the value of having 
researchers, engineers, and artists working within the same building. He hopes to 
preserve that shared environment while helping more people access the tools they need 
for their work.
Please join us in welcoming Swetnam to the CARC community!
To learn more about his background and research, visit tysonswetnam.com
